C-43-李冠儒

從存儲到記得：以阿斯曼文化記憶理論反思世界記憶與我國文資

李冠儒

關鍵字：文化記憶、世界記憶、口述傳統、文化資產哲學

摘 要

明年（2027）適逢教科文組織「世界記憶計畫」成立三十五周年，本文以阿萊達・阿斯曼《回憶空間：文化記憶的形式和變遷》為理論基礎，反思何謂世界記憶、文化記憶，以及記憶、回憶與遺忘三者的關係，並據以檢討我國「臺灣世界記憶國家名錄」與無形文資「口述傳統」。本文先比較世界記憶與世界遺產，凸顯世界記憶的差異，並指出世界記憶面臨三項問題：歐洲中心主義、政治化以及與世界遺產的差別待遇。其次，以阿斯曼的理論說明，何謂文化記憶？其功能區分為「功能記憶」與「存儲記憶」，兩者互為表裡、辯證循環。又何謂記憶ˋ回憶與遺忘的關係？回憶發生在當下，記憶則是過去，回憶必然伴隨遺忘，遺忘是記憶更新的條件。最後，本文就制度與實務提出三點建議：第一，國家名錄應著重教育推廣、公共空間營造，並納入文資體系，成為接納多元差異回憶的媒介。第二，無形文資不應停留在博物館式保存，而應結合活態傳承與母語振興，將存儲記憶激活為功能記憶。第三，有形文資應由信息性閱讀轉向追念的閱讀，目前文資再利用多僅停留於象徵物，卻缺乏與地點和歷史的連結，應讓人進入回憶空間，激發原初情感。方能落實《文化資產保存法》「充實國民精神生活，發揚多元文化」之宗旨。


From Storage to Remembering: Reflecting on World Memory and Our Cultural Heritage through Assmann’s Theory of Cultural Memory

Kuan-Ju, Lee

KEYWORDS: Cultural Memory, World Memory, Oral Traditions, Philosophy of Cultural Heritage

ABSTRACT

Next year (2027) marks the thirty-fifth anniversary of UNESCO’s Memory of the World Programme. Drawing on Aleida Assmann’s *Cultural Memory and Western Civilization: Functions, Media, Archives*, this article reflects on what constitutes world memory and cultural memory, as well as the relationship among memory, recollection, and forgetting, and uses these reflections to examine Taiwan’s “National Register of the Memory of the World” and the intangible cultural heritage category of “oral traditions.” The article first compares the Memory of the World with the World Heritage programme, highlighting the differences between them, and identifies three issues facing the Memory of the World: Eurocentrism, politicization, and unequal treatment compared with World Heritage. Second, drawing on Assmann’s theory, it explains what cultural memory is. Its functions are divided into “functional memory” and “storage memory,” which are two sides of the same coin and form a dialectical cycle. It also examines the relationship among memory, recollection, and forgetting. Recollection occurs in the present, whereas memory belongs to the past; recollection is inevitably accompanied by forgetting, and forgetting is a condition for the renewal of memory. Finally, the article puts forward three recommendations concerning institutions and practice: First, the national register should emphasize educational outreach and the creation of public spaces, and should be incorporated into the cultural heritage system to become a medium for accommodating diverse and differing recollections. Second, intangible cultural heritage should not remain at the level of museum-style preservation, but should be combined with living transmission and revitalization of mother tongues, thereby activating storage memory as functional memory. Third, tangible cultural heritage should shift from informational reading to commemorative reading. At present, the reuse of cultural heritage mostly remains at the level of symbolic objects, while lacking connections with places and history. People should be enabled to enter spaces of memory and evoke primordial emotions. Only in this way can the purpose of the Cultural Heritage Preservation Act, “enriching the spiritual life of the people and promoting multiculturalism,” be realized.
